Solid-state batteries: hype or breakthrough?

What changes

A solid-state battery swaps the flammable liquid electrolyte of a conventional lithium-ion cell for a solid material such as a sulfide, an oxide ceramic or a polymer. Developers say this can raise energy density to as much as 400 Wh/kg, improve safety because there is no flammable liquid, and allow faster charging.

The obstacles

Lithium dendrites can still penetrate brittle ceramic electrolytes, the contact between solid layers creates high interface resistance, and manufacturing at scale remains expensive. Sulfide electrolytes are also sensitive to moisture and release toxic hydrogen sulfide when damaged.

Timelines

Toyota has said it hopes to start limited production of solid-state cells around 2027-2028. However, most independent analysts do not expect mass-market solid-state batteries before 2030, and some believe the cost will stay above that of today's lithium-ion cells for years after launch.

For example, early solid-state cells are likely to appear first in premium vehicles and consumer electronics, where a higher price can be absorbed.
